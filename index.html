Funding & Property FAQs

30 quick answers · tap any question to expand.
1. Is RO’Lyfe the lender?

No. RO’Lyfe is a funding and real-estate referral/brokerage resource. The applicable lender or provider makes all credit decisions.

2. Does completing this snapshot guarantee funding?

No. It is a preliminary organizer, not an application approval or commitment. Eligibility, underwriting, collateral, documentation and provider rules apply.

3. Will checking my options affect my credit?

This snapshot does not run a credit inquiry. A provider may use a soft or hard inquiry; confirm the type and timing directly before authorizing it.

4. What credit score do I need?

There is no single RO’Lyfe minimum. Requirements vary by product, lender, business profile, revenue, collateral and guarantor.

5. Can a startup apply?

Some programs consider startups, but may require a business plan, owner experience, personal credit, cash injection, collateral or other support.

6. How long must my business be operating?

Requirements vary. Some products consider newer businesses; revenue-based and bank-statement programs often look for operating history.

7. What counts as annual sales?

Use gross business revenue for the most recent completed 12-month period, not personal income or profit. A provider may request bank statements or tax returns to verify it.

8. What if my business has no website?

A website is not universally required. Providers may ask for other evidence of business activity, such as invoices, storefront details or business profiles.

9. What is revenue-based financing?

A business receives capital and repays according to an agreed percentage or schedule tied to revenue. Compare total repayment, fees, holdbacks and daily or weekly cash-flow impact.

10. What is an SBA 7(a) loan?

A U.S. Small Business Administration-guaranteed loan made by participating lenders for eligible business purposes. The lender and SBA rules determine eligibility and terms.

11. What is an SBA 504 loan?

A financing structure commonly used for eligible owner-occupied fixed assets, involving a participating lender and Certified Development Company. Confirm current SBA rules and eligible uses.

12. Can SBA funds be used to buy investment property?

SBA real-estate financing generally requires eligible business occupancy and use. Pure passive investment property is typically not eligible; verify the specific program rules.

13. What is a business line of credit?

A revolving credit facility that lets an eligible business draw, repay and potentially draw again up to a limit. Interest, fees and renewal terms vary.

14. What is equipment financing?

Financing used to acquire eligible equipment. The equipment may serve as collateral, and down payment, term, rate and documentation depend on the provider.

15. What is commercial real-estate financing?

Loans secured by or used to acquire eligible commercial property. Lenders evaluate property value, cash flow, borrower experience, occupancy and exit strategy.

16. What is a DSCR loan?

A debt-service-coverage-ratio loan evaluates a property's income relative to its debt service. Rental income, expenses, property type and lender calculations matter.

17. What is fix-and-flip financing?

Short-term property financing for acquisition and sometimes rehabilitation. Loan-to-cost, loan-to-after-repair-value, experience, liquidity and exit plan can affect terms.

18. What is a bridge loan?

Short-term financing intended to bridge a timing or transaction gap. Costs can be higher than long-term financing, so plan a realistic repayment or refinance exit.

19. What is private money?

Capital from a private lender or investor under negotiated terms. Review the written agreement, collateral, fees, maturity, default terms and applicable law.

20. What documents might a provider request?

Common requests include business formation records, ownership details, identification, bank statements, tax returns, debt schedule, financial statements, invoices and property documents. Submit sensitive records only through an authorized secure provider portal.

21. What is a soft credit inquiry?

A review that generally does not affect a consumer credit score. Confirm whether a provider's inquiry is soft or hard and whether it applies to owners or the business.

22. What is a hard credit inquiry?

A credit inquiry associated with a credit application that may affect a consumer score. Review the provider's disclosure and authorize it only when you understand the impact.

23. Can poor credit be offset by strong revenue?

Some providers weigh revenue, cash flow, collateral or other factors, but no factor guarantees approval. Weak credit can still restrict options or increase costs.

24. Does RO’Lyfe charge an upfront fee to guarantee approval?

No approval or funding result is guaranteed. Review any service or referral fee in writing before agreeing to it, and be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed funding.

25. How quickly can funding arrive?

Timing varies by provider, product, documentation, underwriting and closing. Marketing estimates are not guarantees; confirm the actual timeline in writing.

26. What is cash to close?

The funds the borrower must bring to complete a transaction, including down payment, closing costs, reserves, deposits and other required charges, less applicable credits.

27. What is ARV?

After-repair value is an estimate of a property's market value after planned work is completed. Support it with relevant, dated comparable sales and a realistic scope of work.

28. What should I verify before bidding at an auction?

Confirm sale status and terms, deposits, deadlines, buyer premiums, title and lien issues, redemption rights, occupancy, access, condition, taxes and possession rules with official sources.

29. Are property records saved to the cloud?

No. The current property workspace uses this browser's local storage. Export backups; records do not automatically sync to another device or browser profile.

30. How do I speak with RO’Lyfe?

Email richman@rootoflyfe.com or book a strategy session through the Zoho Bookings link on this page. A consultation is not a lender approval or legal opinion.

Input guide: ARV is your evidence-backed expected value after repairs. Purchase / bid is the amount you expect to pay. Rehab is the repair budget. Closing / auction costs include transaction fees and buyer premiums. Holding costs cover taxes, insurance, utilities and carrying expenses while you own it. Selling costs estimate exit expenses as a percentage of resale price. Contingency adds a reserve for unexpected rehab costs. Target profit is the minimum profit you want to preserve. ARV confidence describes how well the estimate is supported by comparable sales.
