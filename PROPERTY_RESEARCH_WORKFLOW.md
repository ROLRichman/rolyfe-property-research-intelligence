# Property Research Workflow

## Stage 1 — Lead capture
Record:
- Source platform and exact URL
- Auction ID / county / sale date / current status
- Full address and parcel/APN
- Property type (verify detached single-family)
- Opening bid / asking price / deposit / premium
- Deadline, bidder registration, and document requirements

## Stage 2 — Confirm identity
Cross-check address, parcel number, county, unit designation, property type, acreage, zoning/use, and ownership records. Stop if the auction listing and public records appear to describe different properties.

## Stage 3 — Value and comps
- Review Zillow and Redfin as secondary sources.
- Find recent closed comparable sales with similar property type, size, condition, location, and date.
- Prefer sourced sold comps over automated estimates.
- Estimate ARV conservatively and assign confidence: High / Medium / Low.
- Record why each comp is relevant and what differs.

## Stage 4 — Official records
Check county assessor/tax collector, recorder/register of deeds, court docket, sheriff/tax-claim notice, zoning, permits where available, flood maps, and applicable HOA or municipal records. Save URLs, dates, and copies of official notices.

## Stage 5 — Sale-law classification
Identify the exact sale type and governing jurisdiction. Verify:
- Whether buyer receives a lien, certificate, deed, or conditional interest
- Redemption period and process
- Which liens survive or may be discharged
- Title search/quiet-title requirements
- Occupancy, possession, eviction, and access rules
- Deposit, buyer premium, payment deadline, deed timing, and refund rules

If unclear, mark **LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED** and do not treat the property as offer-ready.

## Stage 6 — Physical due diligence
Determine whether inspection is permitted. Check roof, foundation, water intrusion, fire damage, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, septic/well, utilities, structural concerns, environmental hazards, access, and winter maintenance. Use a qualified inspector/contractor when possible.

## Stage 7 — Underwriting
Calculate:
- Purchase price / winning bid
- Buyer premium and auction fees
- Closing, title, legal, recording, and transfer costs
- Taxes, utilities, insurance, HOA, and carrying costs
- Rehab estimate and contingency
- Financing costs and reserves
- Expected exit costs
- Conservative ARV and MAO (maximum allowable offer)

Do not rely on appreciation or optimistic rent assumptions to make an unsafe deal appear profitable.

## Stage 8 — Decision status
- NEW LEAD
- RESEARCHING
- VERIFY SALE STATUS
- TITLE / LEGAL REVIEW
- INSPECTION / CONTRACTOR REVIEW
- UNDERWRITING
- LENDER REVIEW
- OFFER / BID READY
- PASS
- WON — POST-SALE DUE DILIGENCE
- CLOSED
- ARCHIVED

## Stage 9 — Partner handoff
Prepare a concise, factual packet for authorized partners: property summary, evidence links, sale terms, comps, cost model, rehab questions, unresolved risks, and requested next step. A proof-of-funds document is sensitive and should only be shared securely with an intended recipient when appropriate.

## Stage 10 — Outreach
Only prepare campaigns after facts are checked and the offer, assignment, marketing, and outreach methods have been reviewed for applicable legal, contractual, and platform requirements. Avoid misleading “guaranteed funding,” guaranteed returns, or guaranteed availability claims.
